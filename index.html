I built PostIQ for myself. Opened it because more people needed it.

When I was deep in the weeds managing social for accounts that had grown to millions of followers, Buffer was already where publishing happened. I kept a separate notes doc for planning. I reviewed content over email. I tracked what was approved and what wasn't in a separate workflow.

Connecting that workflow to Buffer changed the shape of the tool I wanted — a planning calendar that showed my whole month, a composer that sent directly to my queue, a content pillar system so I always knew what to write, and an approval flow that kept clients in the loop without giving them my Buffer login.

I built it for myself first. Then I thought about how many creators and social managers were running the same patched-together workflow. So I cleaned it up, kept it focused, and put it out there.

PostIQ isn't trying to replace Buffer. It's the planning and strategy layer that makes Buffer feel complete.
